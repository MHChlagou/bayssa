Generated by `npm run content:audit` on 2026-11-22 from `content/dataset`. Do not edit by hand.

## What "verified" means here

Every check in this report is mechanical. A script compared letters between sources. No scholar has reviewed any entry. The status column therefore reads "mechanically verified", never "verified by a scholar", and every entry is still owed a read-through by a person qualified in hadith.

The mechanical checks establish that the wording exists in the cited collection under the cited number, and which grading a named scholar gave that narration in the dataset. They do not establish that the narration is the one the author of Hisn al-Muslim relied on, that the occasion attached to it is right, or that the grading in the third-party dataset was transcribed correctly.

Each entry is described on four separate lines, which must not be read as one verdict:

- Text match: exact or approximate, a comparison of letters by a script.
- Reference check: whether the collection shown agrees with the author's own footnote, and whether the hadith number is the only candidate.
- Scholarly review: none for every entry at this time.
- Publication status: published, or held from production by decision.

## Findings from the review

The engineer read every entry in the first five groups below and every entry flagged by a cross-check. The main findings:

1. Two references are wrong. `h-87` cites Sahih al-Bukhari 93, which is a different narration. `h-166` lists Abu Dawud 2160, which is a supplication for a different occasion. Both passed because the matcher compares wording only.
2. The matcher accepts words that occur in order but not side by side, and it does not know the occasion of a narration. Short or common phrases can therefore match a narration about something else. The "ambiguous number" check counts how many entries are exposed to this.
3. Where a collection holds several narrations with the wording, the number shown was picked by closest wording. It may not be the narration the author cites. The author's footnotes give volume and page of printed editions, so they cannot confirm a hadith number.
4. `h-32` is published although about 41% of its words are bracketed additions that were never matched.
5. Texts are displayed exactly as transcribed by hisnmuslim.com, including typing errors such as أَسْاَلُ in `h-165`. The check against the second transcription lists where the two differ.
6. Gradings are taken from one third-party dataset. For entries where the scholars recorded there disagree, the app shows only the first one in a fixed order (al-Albani, then al-Arna'ut, then Zubair Ali Za'i) and does not show the disagreement.
7. Several entries are complete prophetic statements about a dhikr ("whoever says ... will have ..."), shown in a card with a repetition counter as if the whole statement were recited.
8. The footnote comparison aligns items by text. Where the same dhikr appears in several chapters of the book, the footnote of another chapter may have been picked, so a "not named in the footnote" flag can itself be wrong.

Findings 1 and 4 have since been acted on by decision: see "Decisions recorded". The remaining recommended actions are proposals.
